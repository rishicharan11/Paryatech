A proposal should make a trip easy to understand and the next decision easy to make.

Start with the traveller’s priorities.

Before presenting a day-by-day plan, briefly reflect the request back to the traveller. Explain the pace, the experiences they care about and the choices that shaped your recommendation. That introduction turns a list of services into a trip with a clear purpose.

Make the practical details easy to find.

Keep dates, group size, room arrangements, transfers and inclusions close to the itinerary. State exclusions and any conditions that need a decision. If an option changes the price or availability, make the difference easy to compare rather than burying it in a long message.

Use one visual language.

Your website, proposal and booking documents should feel like they come from the same agency. Reuse your logo, colours and typography consistently. Let the destination imagery support the story, and give important details enough breathing room to be read on a phone.

Review every draft.

AI can help turn a request into a first itinerary, but your team still needs to check the details. Review supplier information, travel times, dates and costs before sharing anything with a traveller. Your judgement is what connects a polished document to a workable trip.
