One shared conversation. A team in sync.

A good handover should move the work forward without making the traveller repeat themselves.

In this story

  1. Make the conversation visible.
  2. Give ownership a name.
  3. Keep internal context internal.
  4. Build a considerate reply library.
  5. Keep the team in one place.

Make the conversation visible.

When a traveller’s messages live on one person’s phone, the whole team depends on that person being available. Start by agreeing which channels your agency uses for client conversations and where the team should look for the complete picture.

Give ownership a name.

A shared inbox works best when a conversation has a clear owner. Assign a teammate to respond, and make it obvious when a conversation is waiting for a client, a supplier or an internal decision. Visibility helps the team avoid both missed replies and duplicate replies.

Keep internal context internal.

A short internal note can explain a supplier constraint, a traveller preference or a promise already made. Write notes for the next person who might handle the enquiry. Keep them factual, useful and connected to the trip so colleagues do not have to search a separate group chat.

Build a considerate reply library.

Save answers to the questions your agency receives often, then adapt each reply to the traveller. A template is a starting point. Check dates, names, inclusions and the latest context before sending it. Consistency should still leave room for the care that makes your agency distinctive.
