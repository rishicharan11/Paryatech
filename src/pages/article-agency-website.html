Agency websites · 4 min read

A website that starts a better conversation.

Help a visitor understand your agency and share enough context for a useful first reply.

In this story

  1. Say who you help, clearly.
  2. Give inspiration a useful next step.
  3. Ask for enough context to reply.
  4. Keep the journey familiar on mobile.
  5. Close the gap after the click.

Say who you help, clearly.

A visitor arriving on your homepage should be able to understand what kind of trips your agency helps arrange. Use specific, accurate language about your work and the travellers you serve. A short explanation is easier to act on than a long collection of travel slogans.

For example, an agency focused on customised family holidays can make that clear alongside relevant trip examples. Keep claims within what your team can support. If a destination page is an inspiration piece rather than a currently available package, label it accordingly so the visitor knows what a conversation with you will establish.

Give inspiration a useful next step.

A striking destination photograph can create interest, but the visitor still needs to know how to continue. Place a clear enquiry action near the content that prompted it. Use a label that describes what will happen, such as discussing a trip, rather than a vague instruction to discover more.

Keep the route consistent across the site. If a traveller enquires from a particular destination page, make it easy for them to mention that interest. Do not imply an instant booking or guaranteed availability when the next step is a conversation with your team. The action should match the process you can deliver.

Ask for enough context to reply.

An initial enquiry form should help your team begin a useful discussion. Consider the traveller’s name, preferred contact details, destination interests, approximate dates and group size. Make room for an undecided answer where that is realistic. Someone early in planning may need your help to narrow the options.

Explain required fields and keep the form manageable on a phone. Ask only for information appropriate to an initial conversation, following your agency’s existing information-handling practices. When testing, check that errors explain how to fix the field and that the final action clearly describes what happens after the enquiry is sent.

Keep the journey familiar on mobile.

Read the site on a small screen as if you had just received its link in a message. Can you read the main offer without zooming? Are contact actions easy to find? Do destination images leave enough room for the words that explain them?

Check the full enquiry route, including any external booking or contact page. Open navigation, complete the form with test information and inspect the confirmation or handover step. Use an authorised test route rather than creating a genuine customer request. A polished homepage is only one part of the visitor’s experience.

Close the gap after the click.

Decide where each website enquiry arrives and who reviews it. The first reply should acknowledge the request and use the details the visitor has already supplied. Asking someone to repeat the whole form can make the website feel disconnected from the agency behind it.

Review actual questions your visitors ask and use them to improve the relevant page. If people repeatedly misunderstand what a sample itinerary includes, clarify the wording near that itinerary. Keep destination information and contact details current. A useful agency website develops through the conversations it starts, with each improvement making the next conversation easier to begin.

The next step should be as clear as the destination.
