Enquiries · 3 min read

A clearer path from hello to booked.

A useful enquiry pipeline starts with one simple question: what needs to happen next?

In this story

  1. Give every enquiry a home.
  2. Choose a clear next step.
  3. Keep the handover short.
  4. Review the quiet enquiries.
  5. Bring the workflow together.

Give every enquiry a home.

A traveller may first write on WhatsApp, follow up by email and return through your website. Treat those messages as one conversation about one trip. Keep the destination, travel dates, group size and important preferences together so your team can pick up the enquiry without asking the traveller to start again.

Choose a clear next step.

A long list of leads is not the same as a working pipeline. For every active enquiry, record the next action and who owns it. It might be a call to clarify the budget, a request for a supplier rate or a proposal waiting for review. Make the action specific enough that a teammate knows what to do.

Keep the handover short.

Before passing an enquiry to a colleague, leave a useful note: what the traveller wants, what you have already promised and what still needs an answer. A shared record reduces the need to forward screenshots and reconstruct a conversation from memory.

Review the quiet enquiries.

Set aside a regular moment to review proposals awaiting a decision and conversations that have gone quiet. Follow up with context: answer an open question or offer a sensible next step. Useful follow-ups feel like service, not a repeated sales message.
